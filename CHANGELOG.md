# Changelog

One entry per release, newest first. A release is a commit on `main` in which `VERSION` in
`server.py` was raised; tag that commit `v<VERSION>` and add its entry here in the same change.
Between releases, changes are collected under "Unreleased". The release number alone does not
identify the code that is running: Settings > Data handling, saved interviews and transcript
exports also carry the build (a desktop release's commit, or `dev+` and a source hash for a
working tree). See [docs/DESKTOP.md](docs/DESKTOP.md#which-build-is-running).

## Unreleased

- A topic opened from **Notes** now appears inside the Workspace panel instead of a dialog over the page, so the voice and transcript stay in view and usable. It shows the current understanding, what is still open and compact sources, and steps through the 16 topics with the arrows, the arrow keys or a strip that shows each topic's coverage. Esc returns to the list.
- A workflow step selected on a diagram now opens in the same kind of panel as a topic: beside the Workspace on Interview, and docked in the map's frame on Review, in an expanded map and in Cinematic, where the map makes room for it when it is wide enough. Neither is a dialog over the page any more: the map stays usable, selecting another step updates the panel, and the arrows or arrow keys step through the map in order. A cited document passage opens with the same header and type.
- The report's Contents keeps the current section highlighted when you select it or scroll through the report.
- Save copy in the Workspace menu uses its tooltip for help.
- Fixes from the November 19 stress test:
  - Live voice is given a glossary of fund terms, the process name and the configured keywords (never words from its own captions, which may be the mishearings). In the same spoken test the interviewer no longer repeated a misheard term back ("the Kike checks" before), and live-voice notes write the listed term while their quotes stay as captioned. The live session sends no new field, and the glossary is left out rather than refuse a start that fits without it.
  - In live voice, notes no longer wait for a long answer to end. Once about 600 characters of what the participant has said (some 40 seconds) are missing from the notes, the notes are updated from the transcript so far while they keep talking, at most once every 30 seconds. The interviewer is still told nothing until the answer ends, and the update for the whole answer follows as before. In the test, the notes of an 18-second answer had arrived 94 seconds later because a 71-second answer began 2.4 seconds after it; in a simulation of that case at an ordinary pace they are now requested 22 seconds into the longer answer.
  - Dictation asks for money amounts in words, also when no spelling hints are set. "$4,250,000.17" had come back as "$4,250,017" every time; it came back right in 6 of 6 tries, and a longer, number-dense answer in 3 of 6 instead of 0. After dictating an amount, in figures or in words, the reminder asks to check every amount.
  - An answer made only of characters the server reads as space (U+001C–U+001F, U+0085) is no longer added; it had locked the interview, because every later request was refused. An answer the server refuses goes back to the reply box, and a saved interview holding such an answer opens without it. A broken character from a paste, in an answer or in the goal, background, process name or owner, becomes U+FFFD instead of being refused. Send is not offered for a box that holds only such characters.
  - A fifth AI request waits up to 8 seconds for one of the four places before it is refused; a draft head start never waits for one. The head start is cancelled by a new answer, Start over and opening another interview, and paused while voice connects; a Create draft that was waiting on a cancelled head start writes its own.
  - The check for changed app files runs in the background, reads only the files the server serves, and reads them again only when one of them changes. A page reload no longer waits for it (1 to 24 seconds while a large file was read or iCloud was syncing), and stray copies such as `app 2.js` no longer change the build or ask for a restart.
  - A large image or PDF page under review holds about half the memory it did: the image is no longer copied while source names are shortened, and a PDF page is read from the open file instead of loading the whole file each time.
  - Clearer messages: a recording that cannot be decoded says so, in dictation and in reviewed voice, and is not offered a retry that cannot work, a CSV with a stray null character names its row (UTF-16 without a byte-order mark is still recognised, also in Cyrillic or Arabic text), and an empty file or request says it is empty.
  - The coverage review of a live-voice interview gets the same glossary, so a note that writes KYC where the caption says "kite" is not treated as an unsupported substitution.
  - `COLLECTOR_NOTES_WORKERS` (1 to 8, default 2) sets how many notes are written at once. On a server shared by several people, two workers never caught up with six interviews answering every 20 seconds. `.env.example` also gave the wrong default for `COLLECTOR_PROVIDER_SLOTS` (24, not 6).
  - In live voice, when the interviewer starts talking while you are still answering and you keep going, also after a short pause for breath, the transcript keeps your whole answer as one turn, with what the interviewer said in one turn after it. The two had been cut into dozens of alternating one-word turns. Once the interviewer has finished speaking, what you say next is a new answer below its question, even if you talked over its first words. Asking to stop now ends voice also when you ask over the interviewer or add "sorry", "thanks" or "have a good day".
  - The banner over the conversation no longer cuts off its first line, names more than three unread files by count, and at phone width the Menu button stays on screen. Typing stays as fast in a 392-turn interview as in a short one.
  - Files are read in a separate process, so a large PDF or workbook no longer slows other interviews. While five large files were read, a typed interview's next question took 1.85 s, as when idle (1.97 s median and up to 2.27 s before), and status requests took at most 11 ms, also as when idle (up to 0.41 s before). Each file's index is unchanged. A reader that crashes, or runs past the 45-second PDF or 90-second workbook limit, is ended and the file shows the usual message; where a separate process cannot start, the file is read as before.
  - A file past a page, sheet, row, column or cell limit is read up to the limit instead of being refused whole, and its card says what was left out: the 2,020-page PDF keeps pages 1 to 500 ("Pages 1 to 500 of 2,020 were read; the rest was not."), the 450,000-row CSV its first 250,000 rows. A file that fills the text index keeps the pages or rows that fit. Safety limits still refuse the whole file, and a file an earlier version refused for its size offers Retry review.

## 0.34.0 · November 19, 2026

- During live voice, Topics can show **Discussing** from the participant's captions. Corrections update that indication immediately. Checked notes and completion still require a source-backed capture.
- Live and typed interviewers can draw on unresolved decisions from earlier answers. In live voice the app delivers a short, attributed lead during a pause and checks whether a newer answer has overtaken it. A resumed call can recover earlier branches and corrections. The interviewer can look up exact wording from this interview or its active reference files.
- Straightforward spoken answers use a smaller notes request that preserves quotations, scope and qualifications. Complex cases and periodic reconciliation use the full capture. Voice can ask the next question throughout. Routine live maps can reuse accepted facts while the route checker still examines their connections.

- **What a file holds reaches the interview in seconds, from every page.** Measured on the files of the November 19 file analysis audit (seconds after upload, before → after): 84-page PDF notes 54.8 → 14.3 with all 84 pages read instead of 48, in-depth review 186 → 18, map 73.5 → 55.5, map source-checked 219 → 103; 44-page PDF notes 39.7 → 10.4; 19-page PDF notes 35.9 → 11.2; workbook notes 35.1 → 18.1 and source-checked map 147 → 80; image notes 39.4 → 21.7; 12-page scan question 167 → 16.1 and notes 193 → 21.7; three files at once question 32.6 → 8.8, notes 62.4 → 18.7 and source-checked map 235 → 102; a 44-page PDF added mid-interview notes 47.3 → 39.5. The question of a text file arrives in 4 to 7.5 seconds and Topic coverage in 6 to 10. What changed:
  - The first notes from a file added before anything is noted or said read the whole file in parallel parts of about 7,000 characters, each with a share of one account's fact budget, beside two requests for the account-wide fields and a topic sweep that catches a topic no part recorded. A part names a fact's passage by line numbers and the passage is cut from the file's own text, so every quotation is exact. The joined reply passes the same ledger checks as before; when a part fails twice the single request is sent as before (`file_parts.py`). On the 84-page file the notes held 20 to 21 of the 23 answer-key facts (17 before) and all 16 topics, with no fact flagged for a missing passage.
  - A file's in-depth batches are sent together, up to 16 at a time, and recorded in file order; the pages of a scan are read together; every review request is on the fast tier; and a file's question is released once its pages and images have been read instead of waiting for the whole review. A file estimated at more than 48 requests (12 before) stops after its first pass.
  - Every structured reply is written on one line and cites short source names that the app maps back (`LEAN_REPLY_NOTE`), about a fifth fewer tokens to write for the same content. The map's source check after a file is added runs on the fast tier.
  - `COLLECTOR_PROVIDER_SLOTS` defaults to 24 (was 6) and four files are reviewed side by side (was two).
  - Fixes found on the way: a workbook cell comment no longer marks its tab as an unread drawing (extraction version 5; stored workbooks are re-checked locally, their sections and notes unchanged); the reviewer's notes filling up, and the in-depth pass adding search queries, no longer count as a change of the files, which threw away a map or notes drawn while the review finished.
  - Not changed: the model, its reasoning setting and what it is asked to find; spreadsheet cell text (notes cite it and the source viewer reads it). The map is still drawn by one request, 20 to 60 seconds.

- **The home screen is one page with one action.** Before an interview starts, the stage fills the window: the GPFS mark, "Let's document your process.", one sentence that says to start with existing documents, and an Upload documents button. A file can be dropped anywhere on the page; the page shows a frame and the button reads Release to upload. Start talking, Type instead and See an example share one quiet line below. Behind the text, a line drawing shows loose documents on the left becoming a process map on the right; it is drawn once, then a slow pulse follows the route, and each side comes forward when its action is pointed at. The drawing is decorative, is hidden from assistive technology, stays still under reduced motion and is left out below 760 pixels of width. The process name, the Voice, Transcript and Workflow tabs and the Workspace rail appear when the interview begins. This replaces the illustrated, dashed upload card.
- **A file added from the home screen is read and asked about in seconds.** A file uploaded before a process was named used to be set aside as "Needs your direction": its reviewer had no stated purpose to compare it with, so the interview opened with the generic question and no notes (six of six test uploads). The first review of such a file now judges the file by its own content; every other review is asked exactly what it was asked before. A first review also publishes its verdict, summary and question as soon as they are written, while its observations follow in the same reply, so the file's question, the first notes and the first map start two to four seconds earlier. The first review and those three requests are sent on the provider's priority tier: the same model and request, written about twice as fast and billed at the provider's priority rate (`COLLECTOR_FAST_TIER=off` turns it off; a project without the tier is served the ordinary way). Topic coverage shows which topics the file has something on while the notes are still being written, and the page asks for results more often while someone is waiting for them. Measured on a four-page PDF with no process name: question after 6.4 s (was 12 to 14 s and generic), topic states after 8.9 s (was 66 s), notes after 31 s (was 66 s), map after 60 s (was 107 s).
- **Settings is a larger window with a clearer layout.** It opens up to 1280 by 900 pixels (from 1040 by 800). The title, setting finder and sections sit in a sidebar, grouped as General, Workspace and Support; each section pins its heading and lays its controls out in cards beside a short explanation. Appearance shows a miniature of the workspace in each theme. The voice list opens over the page instead of pushing the switches down. AI usage shows the share of each limit used, and its bar changes color past 75% and 90%. Application logs are one compact list with search beside the session filter. Help & about lists the keyboard shortcuts. A search with no match suggests searches that do match. No preference, storage or request behavior changed. [Settings experience](docs/SETTINGS_EXPERIENCE.md)
- **More formats on Review is a menu.** It is a button beside Download report whose choices (BPMN, step table, diagram, print) open in a floating panel and say what each one produces. Opening it no longer moves the heading and buttons, and it closes on a choice, on Escape and on a click elsewhere.

A question that only asks what comes next is put back to the model once. On the same personas the final run had 8 generic questions and 7 strong ones (average 3.0), a question still in a median of 3.1 seconds. The live voice (`gpt-live-1`), tested in a browser with synthesized speech as the microphone, went from following the last thing said to picking up a manually handled exception, a late email from legal and a rule nobody enforces, and still answered within a couple of seconds.

Notes for the reviewer say what to look at again in a reader's words, begin with one statement that the draft is AI-generated, and no longer describe how the draft was produced. - **Files.** Add files works after viewing the example and choosing Start your own. Excerpts from a hidden workbook sheet say “hidden sheet”.
- **Recorded answers.** The process name and the names, acronyms and codes the participant has already used are sent to the transcriber as spelling hints.

Fixes for the thirteen findings of the November 16 process-capture run. [Findings, acceptance checks and replay cases](docs/testing/runs/2026-11-16-process-capture/README.md)

- **File review no longer stalls on “Reading your files.”** Status polling continues between a file being indexed and its review reporting a phase, and for as long as a file intake is waiting on its files. A file left indexed without a review phase is reviewed on the next poll. (Finding 1)
- **A map is checked for parts its route passes by before it is published.** A check, a selection or a hold that no connection reaches, and a step whose stated input no route supplies, are found without reading any source. The sources are asked once how those parts join; a connection is added only with a quoted passage from a source it cites, and a direct arrow is rerouted only when the verified connections lead through the part that was detached. What the sources do not settle is named in the open questions. The routing policy now says that a gate lies on the route it governs and that one statement listing actions in order reports that order. (Finding 2)
- **A working map is no longer lost to its own source audit.** The audit of a transcript-only map ran into a 120-second limit, and its 12,000-token allowance was used up by reasoning before it could answer, which discarded maps that had been drawn correctly (14 of 17 in the run). The audit now has 180 seconds when the page waits for it and 240 in the background, with a 24,000-token allowance; a background map has 180 seconds to be drawn.
- **Wrap up releases the synchronized notes without waiting for the map.** A map that is still queued or being drawn, or that failed, keeps its own status and its own retry; the page fetches it when it arrives and applies only the map. A failed map no longer fails Wrap up. (Finding 3)
- **The first question after a file is added is asked from the file's own excerpts**, as soon as the file has been read. The notes and the audited map of the same revision are completed in the background and are dropped when the account has moved on. A file-only start now gets a map as well. (Finding 4)
- **A citation a model got wrong is settled internally.** An operating detail that cites a source outside its step, or wording its source does not contain, is dropped; a source ID that is not a source is taken off; a step or connection left with no source is left off the map and named in the open questions. The map is no longer refused for it, and a file follow-up falls back to the file review's own excerpt-bound question when the model's reply cannot be asked. Harmless reviewer slips (a discard of something never proposed, a discard beside a replacement, one skipped route row) no longer fail a map. (Finding 5)
- **An accepted map ends an earlier map failure in both panels.** A failure of the map alone is shown in Workflow with its retry; Notes says the notes are caught up. (Finding 6)
- **The interviewer checks its question against the answers, including the one just given.** It is given the sentences in which the participant said a detail is unknown or unconfirmed, commits to the one detail it is after before wording the question, and is told not to ask again for a stated unknown or for what an answer already explains. (Finding 7)
- **One capture no longer contradicts itself.** Every structured link to another process is stated in the Connections note; an answer that states a requirement or names another process reopens the open gaps of those topics for an explicit decision. (Finding 8)
- **The same handoff described twice is one link** with both citations; a link whose outcome differs stays separate. (Finding 9)
- **Records and receiving processes are not turned into people.** The question and capture policies keep supporting records, the process that receives them and the people who handle them apart. (Finding 10)
- **The interview goal is not recorded as the purpose of the work.** A purpose statement that rests on the intake alone is dropped when evidence states the purpose, and is otherwise the open gap it is. (Finding 11)
- **A PDF page of text with rules, table grids, shaded bars and bullets is no longer flagged as unread graphics**, also when the exporter puts the whole page inside one form object. Images, shadings, slanted lines, arrowheads, large curves and pages with little text of their own still get a visual reading. Files indexed by an earlier build are compared locally, free of charge, and offered for re-indexing when their sections differ (`EXTRACTION_VERSION` 4). (Finding 12)
- **Indexed text, supplied sections, review progress and unread visuals are separate facts** in what the notes are given about a file, as they are in Assets. (Finding 13)
