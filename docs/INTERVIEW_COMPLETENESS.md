# Evidence and interview completion

The interview is done when the participant confirms an independently reviewed account that is ready for the process owner. Every relevant dimension is supported, explicitly inapplicable, or has a reviewed owner follow-up. The follow-up identifies the missing decision, affected scope, current action, resolving role or source, and participant evidence. The missing fact stays open. If someone would still have to guess how work proceeds or waits, the interviewer asks a focused question or retains a partial account. The [completion gate](AGENT_TEAM.md#completion-belongs-to-the-application) governs the decision.

The current managed-team implementation is described in [AGENT_TEAM.md](AGENT_TEAM.md). Version 0.22.0 uses one lead call for ordinary turns, explicit parallel review only at completion readiness, and zero further calls for an unchanged final confirmation. Candidate topics are provisional; every required dimension and the practical process must pass current independent review before the final omissions check. New substantive evidence invalidates the prior completion authority. Managed review may publish an explicitly cited repair when a summary omitted facts already supplied. A repair is refused when it drops a participant turn the section cited or fails the wording guard below, is applied only after the process reviewer has read the repaired account without raising a further material finding, and is shown to the user with the notes it replaced; that it adds no unreported fact is asked of the reviewer by prompt and checked by the process reviewer, not proved by code ([details](AGENT_TEAM.md#evidence-and-official-coverage)).

Version 0.24.0 brings GPT-Live back as the spoken front (`VOICE_MODE=live`, the default) while the managed team runs on the transcript behind it: the lead captures each spoken answer in the background, the application records and issues the acknowledgement, the final omissions check and the completion line, and the completion permit is unchanged. Version 0.21.0 had replaced direct live audio with reviewed turns, which remain available as `VOICE_MODE=reviewed`. The following 0.20 description is historical; its per-turn review timing does not describe the current UI.

Version 0.20.0 makes topic coverage depend on an explanation of the relevant process facts. Mentioning a topic, naming a tool, writing a heading or reaching the end of one line of questioning is insufficient.

## Coverage assessment

`interview_coverage.py` defines the sixteen-topic rubric and the required dimensions of each topic. The same concise rubric reaches typed interviewing, the question auditor, delegated voice reasoning, workflow capture and draft generation. Direct live voice receives the names of each topic's dimensions without their explanations (`live_topic_guide` in `server.py`, generated from the same table), and a silent note that lists the topics still open by key; the reviewer's gap wording and its suggested question are not sent to the spoken interviewer, and a targeted question returned by a failed completion review is not spoken in live mode.

When a capture proposes any `captured` or `not_applicable` finding, an independent model assessment checks all sixteen topics against the complete supplied transcript and authorized document excerpts. It returns a plain-language explanation, source references, dimension-level support, unresolved gaps and a next useful question. Topic-level and dimension-level non-applicability require explicit participant support. An unavailable detail stays unresolved.

The server validates the assessment and only allows it to demote findings. It cannot promote partial topics, overwrite the supported account or add workflow facts. Missing dimensions, unsupported evidence, documentary-only current-practice claims and unresolved gaps prevent full coverage. If the assessment fails, claimed full topics become partial while useful process capture remains available. A model can still misinterpret valid evidence; structural validation is not semantic proof or independent process verification.

Uncertainty is scoped to the relevant finding and dimension. An unknown folder in a long answer must not erase an explicitly established accountable role from the same answer. A preparer, reviewer or approver is not automatically the role accountable for the overall result.

A local wording guard backs this up (`interview_coverage.uncertain_wording`). It holds a proposed `captured` or `not_applicable` topic at partial only when the wording reports that something is not known. Its rules state constructions, not sentences:

- A state (unknown, unclear, unverified, not confirmed, not yet known, to be confirmed, `UNKNOWN`, `TBC`) used as the predicate of something: after "is" ("the cutoff is unknown"), or as a note after the thing it is about, however the note is joined and whatever follows it ("Approver: not confirmed at this stage.", "Cutoff - unknown.", "Owner (unknown)", "About 40 a month (exact figure unknown).", "Timing unclear at present.", "Volume not confirmed by the participant.").
- The speaker's limit: "I don't know", "we think", "nobody knows", "cannot say", "as far as the participant knows", "that is a guess".
- A hedge or an assumption in the same clause as who owns, approves or is responsible, whether it stands before, after or apart from it: "probably approves", "Approver: probably the CFO.", "The CFO approves, probably.", "The CFO (probably) approves.", "The approver may be the CFO.", "Working assumption: the CFO approves.", "The analyst believes the CFO approves.". In the accountability topic any such hedge holds, and in the checks topic a hedged name alone does ("Probably the CFO."). A hedge on anything else ("the cutoff is probably 3 p.m.", "is likely to process 40 notices") does not hold a topic; an attributed estimate is left to the independent review.
- A point left open: "The cutoff needs to be confirmed.", "Whether Treasury accepts the entry needs to be confirmed.", "Open point: who receives the file.", "requires confirmation from the participant", "the team assumes that the rate is fixed".

What is not a gap is stated the same way and set aside before the rules run:

- The word mentioned as a label or value of the process, which no rewording could remove: in a list of values ("the portal shows three states: pending, confirmed, not confirmed", "sorts receipts into matched, unmatched, unknown"), in a legend beside its opposite ("Confirmed: released the same day. Unconfirmed: held overnight."), as part of a name or the gloss of a code ("the Payer (Unknown) bucket", "code 99 (not known)"), in quotation marks after a naming word ("a status called \"unconfirmed\""), or with a capital as the value a status or category takes ("the default category is Unknown"). Quotation marks, a capital or a list alone do not make a label ("The owner is \"unclear\".", "Owner: Unknown." and "Owner, approver, unknown." are held), and a label that stands where an owner or approver should be is a missing answer.
- A handled case: a condition with what happens ("If the sender is unknown, Treasury posts the receipt to suspense", "Sender unknown, Treasury posts the receipt to suspense", "cannot determine which fund applies; the analyst selects it") and a class of items in a state ("receipts whose payer is unknown are held", "the reviewer rejects any wire whose beneficiary is not confirmed"). "When the bank confirms the wire is unknown.", "It goes to someone whose role is unknown.", "Cutoff unknown, Treasury to confirm." and "cannot identify where the file goes next; Treasury was not interviewed." are gaps.
- A step with its party, place or timing: "not confirmed by the bank by 3 p.m.", "not specified in the LPA", "has to be confirmed by a second approver before release", "the owner has to be confirmed by the steering group each year", "the amount is unknown until the bank file loads", "every payment needs to be confirmed". What waits on asking somebody is not a step: "unknown until the follow-up interview", "not confirmed in the interview", "the owner needs to be confirmed before the report is final".
- The word before the noun it describes ("unknown receipts", "unconfirmed trades") and a convention with its source ("the model assumes that fees accrue daily", "a 365-day year is assumed." in the rules topic, "Assumption: a 30/360 day count per the LPA."). An assumed owner, approval or sign-off is a guess in every topic.

Where the wording alone cannot tell a gap from a plain fact, the guard holds, because the two errors do not cost the same: a held topic names its wording to the lead, who asks about the point or restates the fact, which costs one turn, while a passed one lets an interview complete on a hedged fact. So "The cutoff needs to be confirmed.", "Not confirmed: the analyst calls the bank.", "Wires to Treasury unconfirmed; the analyst chases at noon." and "The analyst escalates receipts that are unconfirmed." are held, and the lead is told the wording. `tests/fixtures/uncertainty_gate_cases.json` is the corpus the rules are held to (every sentence the audit and the reviews named, with its decision, and the rows held by cost with their reasons); `tests/fixtures/uncertainty_gate_heldout.json` holds sentences written after the rules were final, with what was missed when they were written. The same rules check the reviewer's dimension details and the status the browser shows, so the three agree; `tests/test_uncertainty_gate.py` fails if the browser's copy in `web/app.js` differs. When the guard holds a topic, the private ledger records the fact and its exact wording (`held_for_wording`), the fact becomes a reconciliation target, and the lead is told to ask about the point or to restate the known fact plainly. The note stays while the fact is unchanged and the topic is partial. Ordinary concurrent turns rebuild the capture without that ledger, so there the wording is named again from the previous capture: to the lead as `held_wording` (topic and wording, server-owned) and to the question model in its analysis advice (`unresolved_wording`). The guard is lexical and conservative. It is not a semantic proof: it can still hold a confident sentence or pass an implicit doubt, and the independent review decides whether a topic is explained.

Reopened saved sessions retain their answers, sources and workflow, but their previous full-coverage claims are provisional until reassessed. The app offers **Recheck coverage** and also reassesses on continued interviewing. Opening a file alone does not make a provider request. A failed coverage review uses the same explicit retry, without an automatic retry loop. Request deadlines include the added assessment so a slow valid response is not discarded by the previous shorter browser timeout.

## Conversation and closure

The question auditor checks the final wording against the same rubric and full transcript. A deterministic response check runs after it, so the auditor cannot bypass completion checks by replacing the proposed question with recognized wrap-up language. An incomplete manual completeness check names open sections and requests corrections without announcing that the interview is finished.

The interviewer retains both threads when someone mentions two material issues. It switches from an unproductive vague probe to a concrete ambiguity or ordinary example, respects explicitly unavailable details and moves to untouched sections. It distinguishes risk ownership from actual consequences, process names from file names, and known output artifacts from unexplained transitions.

A local “No” answers the preceding local question. It does not confirm all sections. A new exception after a final check revokes confirmation. A reply to the final check that says “no” without adding anything confirms it however it is worded; a reply that neither confirms nor adds anything has the check asked again without a second review; after the completion line a thank-you or a goodbye changes nothing (see “Completion belongs to the application” in [AGENT_TEAM.md](AGENT_TEAM.md)). Explicit participant stops are respected immediately, with the account retained as partial where necessary. A stop in reply to the final check confirms nothing either, and the review that passed is kept for when the participant returns. If all remaining gaps are explicitly unavailable, a partial handoff is appropriate; the sections remain incomplete. Partial drafts remain available.

## Live voice

The browser sends one short note through the silent context channel after a completed capture and on connection, as a single event in the interviewer's own voice: whether the notes are current, the open topic labels, without a suggested spoken question. It is held while a participant caption is in progress. Caption fragments send nothing, and a participant caption is recorded only when the microphone held speech during its timeline window (0.24.1; the earlier eleven-finding JSON snapshot repeated per fragment made the live model voice the participant's answers). New speech invalidates earlier readiness. Incomplete captions, changed documents, unaudited restored captures and lagging analyses cannot authorize completion. These updates are interpretations, never participant evidence, and do not trigger speech.

Live voice instructions forbid any self-initiated closure; the application issues the acknowledgement, the final check and the completion line as exact lines to say, and only its permit completes the interview. The live audio stream still has no deterministic pre-audio content gate on ordinary questions: prompt compliance and physical microphone/speaker turn-taking require live evaluation. Offline transport tests and text-model evaluations do not establish those behaviors.

## Validation approach

Regression tests cover premature replacements from the question auditor, a local “No,” manual checks with gaps, explicit stops, additions after a final check, assessment failures, required dimensions, citation boundaries, scoped uncertainty and stale live snapshots. Synthetic real-model checks test question selection and semantic coverage separately from those contracts. See the dated build verification entry for exact outcomes and limitations.

No fine-tuning or model-weight changes are involved. This release changes the application instructions, evidence assessment, response validation and live context.
