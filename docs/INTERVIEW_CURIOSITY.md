# Purposeful curiosity

The interviewer should understand how the reported process works, rather than collect a list of
step names. A participant saying “I wake up” has named an event; they have not explained what
initiates it. Saying “I check the file” has named an action; it does not establish the check or its
decision criterion. Missing details remain unknown until the participant supplies them.

After each answer, choose one useful, answerable follow-up close to that answer when it fills a
material gap. Prefer it to a generic next-step question when otherwise a reader would have to guess.
Once the relevant detail is understood, continue the normal route or another framework topic.

| Reported detail | Example follow-up | Framework value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I wake up, get dressed and leave.” | “What usually wakes you up?” | Initiating mechanism, without assuming an alarm or person |
| “I check the file, then send it.” | “What do you check in the file?” | Actual action, check and readiness |
| “I attach either a ticket or a document.” | “What determines which one you attach?” | Choice condition and meaningful differences |
| “I send the package for review.” | “How does the reviewer know it is ready?” | Handoff and readiness signal |
| “If the total differs, I park it.” | “What happens to the file when you park it?” | Exception handling and unresolved next state |
| “Compiling it relies on memory.” | “Which part of compiling it depends on memory?” | Manual work and pain point |

These are examples of information needs, not required wording or a script. Questions use the
participant's terms and ask neutrally for the missing fact. They do not turn guessed mechanisms,
actors, controls, reasons or outcomes into established practice.

Curiosity remains bounded by the eleven-topic framework and the practical procedure. It does not
mean asking more questions about everything. Do not re-ask a waking mechanism already explained,
request irrelevant brands/settings or personal trivia, invent hypothetical exceptions, or pressure
a participant to guess after both a detail and its resolving source are unavailable. A stop, pause
or declined question takes precedence. Documentary simulations are questioned as documentary
accounts, without demanding firsthand transactions or claiming employee confirmation.

Typed questions receive the shared questioning, curiosity, correction and selection
policies. Direct Live voice uses its compact conversation policy, selection guidance and a guide to
the sixteen topics. The separate lead captures facts using a notes-only contract on ordinary
Live and concurrent turns. Document follow-ups use the question-bearing capture contract.
The legacy question-audit path remains available for adapters.

Validation combines offline wiring/mutation checks with actual model responses to fictional cases.
Questions are reviewed for the information they seek, relevance, neutrality, novelty and respect
for unknowns and stopping. A valid response schema alone does not establish question quality.
Text replays of the voice prompt are proxies, not proof of an actual voice-provider conversation
or physical microphone/speaker behavior. See [build verification](BUILD_VERIFICATION.md).
